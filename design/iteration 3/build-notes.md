# Iteration 3 — what we're doing and why

Notes on how we're approaching your refinement brief, written so they can be
read alongside the demo. Updated as we work through the sections.

---

## How we're sequencing the work

We're doing this in two passes: **structure, content and typography first,
with placeholder images throughout** — then imagery as a separate pass once
the editorial foundation is agreed.

This follows from your own brief. You've said the typography should become
one of the defining characteristics of the website, and that imagery exists
to support it. Building the type first tests exactly that. If the page reads
well with grey boxes where the photographs go, the structure is genuinely
doing the work. If it needs the photographs to hold together, that's useful
to know now rather than later.

Every image slot is a component with a fixed shape, so dropping a real
photograph in later is a single change and never causes the layout to move.
Each placeholder also carries a short note about what belongs there, which
doubles as a brief when we come to choosing images.

---

## Typography — the four levels

This was your biggest single point, so it's where we started. The previous
version didn't separate the levels enough, which is what made the pages feel
denser than they actually were.

There are now four deliberately distinct levels:

| Level | How it reads |
|---|---|
| **Section heading** | Small, uppercase, in the Ascendiate blue, with a short rule beneath |
| **Lead statement** | Large and medium-weight — the dominant element in every block |
| **Supporting text** | A clear step down in size, but still in full-strength ink |
| **Body text** | Smallest and lightest, in grey, one idea per paragraph |

The important gap is between the lead statement and everything below it. The
lead statement is roughly twice the size of the body copy and sits in
stronger ink, so the eye lands there first every time. Supporting text stays
in ink rather than grey, so it reads as part of the statement rather than as
body copy — that's what creates three tiers rather than two.

**Horizontal dividers.** As you suggested, there's a hairline between the
supporting text and the body copy. It's very light — the intention is a
pause rather than a separator. Where a block has no supporting text, the
divider is omitted rather than left floating.

## Editorial composition — the alternating rhythm

The About section alternates: image left with text right, then text left
with image right, then back again. Approach mirrors it, opening from the
opposite side, so the two sections move in different directions and the page
gains a sense of progression rather than repetition.

The images have been given a wider, shallower shape than before. They were
originally taller, which made them compete with the text. Since the point is
for typography to lead, they've been stepped back.

## The logo and the brand blue

The logo lockup you selected is now in use across the site.

We've also adjusted the site's blue to match the logo artwork exactly. The
previous blue was a slightly lighter, more purple tone than the logo's, which
would have been noticeable wherever the two appeared close together. Every
blue on the site — section headings, rules, links, hover states — now comes
from a single value taken from the logo itself.

## Hero

The eyebrow is gone, the headline is unchanged, the supporting line is your
new wording, and the button now reads Mandates.

**One change worth flagging.** You asked to retain the hero's composition,
but your rough visualisation shows something different from what was there:
text on the left with the image beside it. The previous hero was a
full-width image with the headline laid over the top of it.

We've followed the visualisation. It keeps the light/dark contrast you liked,
but the headline now sits on the light panel rather than over a photograph,
which is what allows the typography to lead. If you did mean to keep the
overlay treatment, it's an easy change back — just say.

The previous hero graphic has been retired. It was the converging-pathways
artwork, which is exactly the aviation-and-digital-networks association your
brief moves away from. That slot is now a placeholder like the others.

## Approach

The same four levels, with the rhythm mirrored against About as described
above.

The placeholder notes step down in emphasis through the section — "defined
form", then "opening out, more understated" — so the imagery becomes quieter
as the narrative moves toward the individual.

**Your portrait** is now built into the final block as one half of the
composition, sitting alongside the closing text rather than beneath it as a
signature. It reads as the conclusion of the section, which is what you
asked for.

One note: the photograph currently in place is a conventional corporate
portrait, and your brief asks for something more editorial. It's in as a
placeholder so the composition can be judged. There's a question about this
in `questions-for-nick.md`.

## Mandates

Renamed from Experience throughout — navigation, section heading, and the
hero button.

The six examples are no longer equal cards. A single central image anchors
the composition, with three mandates either side. The left column is set
ranged right and the right column ranged left, so both lean in toward the
centre and the eye goes to the anchor first. The mandates then read outward
from it as supporting evidence.

Each example now leads with the commercial outcome, in a size clearly above
the supporting evidence beneath it. The evidence lines are your shortened
wording, which does a lot of the work here — the section can be scanned in a
few seconds but still rewards reading properly.

Your revised copy has been used exactly as written. Note that it changes two
things from the previous version: the fuel hedging figure is now c.USD300m
(previously c.USD150m), and the sixth example is now the USD1.25bn takeover
bid rather than the engine leasing business. Worth confirming those are
intentional.

On smaller screens the three columns unwind into one, with the image first,
so the anchor still leads.

## Contact

The details were noticeably smaller than the body copy elsewhere on the site,
which is what made this section feel disconnected from the rest.

Everything is now on the same scale as the other sections: the labels use the
same treatment as the section headings above them, and the address, telephone
and email are set at the same size as supporting text elsewhere. The section
heading now matches About, Approach and Mandates.

The opening statement is set at three lines rather than two. Making it two
would need a much wider line than is comfortable at that size, and you asked
to be told rather than have the type reduced — so there's a question about
the wording in `questions-for-nick.md`.

## Open

### About — the final "Ascendiate" block has no supporting text

The Challenge and Route forward blocks both have all four levels (section
heading, lead statement, supporting text, body text). The Ascendiate block
jumps straight from the lead statement to the body text.

Built as-is, dropping the divider so it doesn't look broken. The effect is
that the closing block is slightly quieter than the two before it — when
it's arguably the one that should land hardest.

**Question:** would you like a supporting line here, or is the shorter block
a deliberate landing point?

### Lead statements and the two-line rule

Your brief asks for supporting statements to stay within two lines, and says
to flag it rather than reduce the font size if copy runs long.

All the current lead statements hold to two lines at desktop width. They are
close to the limit — any lengthening will push them to three and weaken the
rhythm. Flagging now so it's a known constraint on any copy revisions.

**Question:** none needed yet. Noted so we can come back to you rather than
shrink the type.

### Mandates — the centrepiece image and the "not a bridge" note

Your text is clear that the image should communicate the *qualities* of
engineered structures — integrity, convergence, perspective, destination —
rather than a particular structure, and specifically says not a bridge. The
rough visualisation uses a bridge.

**Question:** is the bridge in the visualisation standing in for the idea, or
is it closer to what you actually want than the text suggests?

### Imagery — hero and Mandates could end up looking alike

Your three preferred hero references (C2, A2, C3) are all variations on the
same idea: a concrete corridor with light at the far end and a lone figure.
The Mandates brief asks for symmetry, perspective and a visible vanishing
point — which describes those same images fairly closely.

The risk is the hero and the section centrepiece reading as the same
photograph twice, which would undercut the sense of progression you want.

**Question:** happy for us to deliberately differentiate the two — for
example, keeping the corridor-and-figure idea for the hero and finding
something more overhead or structural for Mandates?

### Contact — the statement runs to three lines

Your two-line guidance is being applied everywhere. The Contact statement is
the one place it doesn't quite fit:

> Ascendiate welcomes the opportunity to discuss how it can help create,
> preserve or unlock value.

At 96 characters it needs roughly 48 characters a line to fall into two,
which is too wide a measure for type at this size — the line becomes hard to
track back. We've set it at three lines rather than reducing the font size.

**Question:** would you like to shorten it? Something around 70 characters
would sit comfortably in two lines. For example:

> Ascendiate welcomes the opportunity to discuss creating, preserving or
> unlocking value.

Entirely your call — three lines reads perfectly well.

**Question:** is there another shot available, or should we plan a new one?

## Answered

### The logo

Nick has selected the lockup in `design/logo`. Now in use across the site.

with placeholder images throughout. Imagery becomes a separate pass once the
editorial foundation is agreed.

placeholder for a photograph is a one-line change and never a re-layout.

## Lead — Editorial (white-on-image hero + tinted hairlines)

This is the concept most directly built from Nick's strongest signals. The Newsreader serif sets an editorial register that matches his repeated praise for "sophisticated, restrained, credible" sites like IBA and Teneo, and the white headline on the dark photographic hero replicates the exact AlixPartners technique he singled out as drawing attention effectively. Pacing is dialled one notch lighter than IBA, addressing his only criticism of that reference.

- Single continuous scroll — directly answers his most repeated dislike (flagged on 7 of 15 sites).
- No icons anywhere — answers a dislike that recurred on 6 sites.
- Hairline section dividers in deep blue at low opacity — borrows the Teneo technique he praised.
- Sentence case throughout, with the wordmark as the only uppercase — answers the "dated and overly corporate" caps complaint (flexjet, vistaglobal).
- Single hero motion moment only — respects his recurring "slightly overdone" qualifier on motion.

## Secondary — Light (calibrated to IBA pacing)

The institutional alternative for a brief that wants to read as consulting rather than editorial. Inter throughout, navy ink on warm off-white, and the Ascendiate blue carried into the primary CTA rather than the hero photograph. The hero is text-first with the atmospheric image sitting *below* the headline as a calmer beat — a deliberate move away from the white-on-image overlay if Nick reads that as too dramatic for an advisory practice.

- Same foundational rules as the Lead (single scroll, no icons, hairlines, sparse density, single motion instance).
- Cleaner consulting register via Inter — closer in feel to ICF and Argon & Co, both of which Nick praised for modern typography.
- Brand blue used selectively and only at high-intent moments (CTA, link underline) — answers his "restrained colour palette" preference and his Livingstone criticism about branding overpowering content.
- Image sits as a supporting element rather than the hero's backdrop — a quieter answer to his "imagery slightly overused" criticism of IBA.

---

## Secondary — Dark (calibrated to IBA pacing, more cinematic)

A low-light executive register for the same brief. Charcoal ground, warm off-white text, and a lifted blue (#8AA3E6) tuned for dark-mode legibility. Contrast is achieved through the palette itself rather than through stacked imagery, which keeps the page restrained even at higher visual weight. This is the most "evening" of the three — closer in mood to Airborne Capital and SMBC, both of which Nick praised for atmosphere while flagging their motion as overdone.

- All Lead constraints carried over (single scroll, no icons, hairlines, sentence case, single motion instance).
- Text-first hero — no overlay drama, so the cinematic feel comes from palette discipline rather than imagery, addressing the "slightly overdone" motion criticism without losing presence.
- Newsreader display retained for typographic consistency with the Lead, so all three concepts read as one family.
- Dusk-toned portrait treatment in About — keeps the founder presence intentional rather than decorative, which matches Nick's positioning of Ascendiate as built on his own track record.

---

**One observation worth flagging to Nick:** all three concepts are deliberately close in structure and constraints — they differ in *register*, not in *rules*. That should be presented as a feature, not a coincidence. The 15-site review produced a very clear set of constraints, and these three concepts test which register (editorial / institutional / cinematic) sits most comfortably on top of those shared constraints. That framing should make his choice feel like a mood decision rather than a structural one.
